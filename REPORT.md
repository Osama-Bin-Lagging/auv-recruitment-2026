# Report

Replace everything below the headings with your own writing. Keep the headings.
Delete any section that doesn't apply, apart from the AI one.

Aim for about two pages. Longer isn't better. We're reading for whether you
understood what you built, not for volume.

Write it yourself. The interview is a conversation about this document, and we
will ask you to expand on anything in it.

---

## Who you are

Name, roll number, and which questions you attempted.

## Q1: Find the Pinger

**What your controller does, and why.** The approach, in a few sentences. Why
this and not something else.

**What didn't work.** Something you tried that failed, how you noticed, and how
you worked out the cause. This is the part we care most about. "It didn't work so
I tried something else" is not an answer. What did you look at? What did it tell
you?

**What you'd do with more time.** Where you know it's weak.

## Q2: Fly the Course

**What your solution does, and why.** How you plan the path, how you handle the
obstacles, how you approach a gate.

**What didn't work.** Same as above. The bridge, the frames and the gate
approach all have obvious failure modes. Pick a real one you hit.

**What you'd do with more time.**

## Q3: Gate Detection

Only if you attempted it. Delete this section otherwise.

Your approach, your augmentation strategy, and why you chose it. If you looked at
the data before training, say what you found.

## Use of AI

Required, even if the answer is "none".

List:

- which tools you used
- which parts of your submission they produced
- what you changed afterwards, and why

Be specific about scope. Naming a tool isn't a declaration. We want to know which
files or functions came from where.

Undeclared use is a problem. Declared use isn't, and this section is not scored
against you.

## What doesn't work

Known limitations. Cases where you know you fail, things you ran out of time for,
anything you're unsure about.

Being straight here counts in your favour. We will find these anyway when we run
your code, and a submission that already knows its own weaknesses reads much
better than one that claims to be finished.
